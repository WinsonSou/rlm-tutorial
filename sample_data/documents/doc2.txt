Title: The Manhattan Project

The Manhattan Project was a secret research program that produced the first nuclear weapons during World War II. It was led by the United States with support from the United Kingdom and Canada. The project began in 1942 and employed over 125,000 people at its peak.

J. Robert Oppenheimer served as the scientific director of the Los Alamos Laboratory, where the bombs were designed and built. The project involved multiple sites across the country, including Oak Ridge, Tennessee for uranium enrichment and Hanford, Washington for plutonium production.

The first nuclear device was tested on July 16, 1945, at the Trinity test site in New Mexico. The explosion yielded approximately 20 kilotons of TNT equivalent. Oppenheimer famously recalled a line from the Bhagavad Gita: "Now I am become Death, the destroyer of worlds."

Two atomic bombs were subsequently dropped on Japan: "Little Boy" on Hiroshima on August 6, 1945, and "Fat Man" on Nagasaki on August 9, 1945. Japan announced its surrender on August 15, 1945.

KEY_DETAIL: The total cost of the Manhattan Project was approximately $2 billion in 1945 dollars (roughly $28 billion in today's money), making it one of the most expensive research programs in history.
